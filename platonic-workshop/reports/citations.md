## How references are read

- **Title and page** (`Republic 509 B`, `Rep. vi. 509 b`, `Laws 892 A, B`, `Phaedo 100D`): a title of one of Plato's works (full, abbreviated, Taylor's names such as *the Banquet*, or Latin such as *Legg.*), then Stephanus numbers. A letter on its own continues the page before it. One- and two-letter abbreviations (`R.`, `Ti.`) are read only with their full stop and a section letter.
- **Page in this dialogue** (`cf. 31 B`, `In 29 E`): a page and letter with no title, read as the note's own dialogue when the page is one of its pages. Not read after `p.`, `vol.`, `l.`, `ch.` or `§`.
- **Title read through OCR** (`Zheaetetus 176 A`): a capitalised word within a letter or two of a title (difflib ratio ≥ 0.8) followed by a page of that work. Always marked uncertain.
- **Dialogue named** (`in the Phaedrus`, `his Republic`): a full title after *the*, *his* or *Plato's*, with no page after it. One link per note and dialogue, to the dialogue as a whole; the note's own dialogue is not counted. Titles that are also ordinary words (*the Laws*, *the Republic*, *the Sophist*, *the Banquet*…) are marked uncertain.
- **The Loeb notes** were read in milestone 2 from the titles their TEI marks; here they are checked, and the dialogues they name without a page are added. A number after another title that is not a page of that work but is one of the note's own dialogue (`Laws 690 A ff. and 434 B` in a note on the Republic) is read as the note's own dialogue, and marked uncertain.

A link is **uncertain** when the title was read through OCR, the page has no section of that letter (the link goes to the page), the page is not in the book named, or a bare reference follows another name (`Athenaeus XI 507 D`), so it may be another author's page. **Unresolved**: the work is named but has no such page; the words are kept, nothing is linked.
