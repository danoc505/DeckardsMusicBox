The Timaeus commentaries of milestone 7 are in `reports/commentary_timaeus.md`.

## Notes headed by their Stephanus reference

Adam, England, Burnet and Bury head each note with the page, letter and line it is on. The note is put at that section. Where the page had to be counted (only the letter is printed, lower than the one before), the Greek the note quotes decides: found word for word there, the count is confirmed; found at one place only on a page either side, the note is put there and says so. Where the Greek is found at the section printed, the printed reference is confirmed by the text itself: that share is the check on the reading of the heads.

Where the quoted Greek is not found, the OCR has usually misread it (Adam's vol. I reads all his Greek in Latin letters), or the editor's text differs from Burnet's. The note stays at the section printed.

## Notes headed by the Greek words alone

Archer-Hind's Phaedo, Campbell's Theaetetus, Sophist and Statesman, and Jowett and Campbell's Republic quote the Greek and close it with a bracket (Lodge's Gorgias with a colon), with the line of their page of Greek but no Stephanus reference; the words are found in Burnet's text, as for Archer-Hind's Timaeus. **Check:** the notes are printed in the order of the text, so a note placed before the note printed before it is out of order; the share in order is the check on the placing.

## Where each book came from

- **Adam (1902)**: The Republic of Plato, edited with critical notes, commentary and appendices by James Adam, 2 vols. (Cambridge: University Press, 1902). Internet Archive OCR of the University of Toronto copy: [republic01platuoft](https://archive.org/details/republic01platuoft), [republic02platuoft](https://archive.org/details/republic02platuoft), in `sources/adam-1902/`. Vol. I's OCR reads Adam's Greek in Latin letters, so its notes are placed by the reference printed at their head only; vol. II reads the Greek well.
- **England (1921)**: The Laws of Plato, the text edited with introduction, notes, etc. by E. B. England, 2 vols. (Manchester: University Press, 1921). Internet Archive OCR of the University of Toronto copy: [thelawsofplatoth01platuoft](https://archive.org/details/thelawsofplatoth01platuoft), [lawsofplatotexte02platuoft](https://archive.org/details/lawsofplatotexte02platuoft), in `sources/england-1921/`.

## Looked for and not imported

- Thompson, Gorgias (1871; gorgiaswithengli00platuoft) and Phaedrus (1868; phaedrusofplato00plat): the only copies found, and their OCR reads all the Greek in Latin letters ('éx tpirwy' for ἐκ τρίτων), lemmata and text alike.
- Adam, Republic, the 1909 reprint (republicplat01plat, republicplat02plat): the 1902 printing is used; vol. I of the reprint reads the Greek no better.
- Archer-Hind, Phaedo, other copies (platonosphaidon00plat, phaedofoplato00platuoft): their OCR breaks the notes; the Cornell copy is used.
- Bury, Philebus (1897; philebus00plat_0, philebusofplato00plat_0): the OCR runs the two columns of notes into each other line by line, so the notes cannot be separated.
- Burnet, Euthyphro, Apology and Crito (1924): only lending-library scans, with no OCR text to download.
- England, Laws, the Digital Library of India copy (dli.ernet.18445): the Toronto copy is used.
- Adam, Euthyphro (1890; euthyphroneuthyp00platuoft): the OCR reads the English of the notes in Greek letters.
- Adam, Crito (1888; critowithintrod00platuoft) and Tatham, Laches (1896; lachesofplato00tath): the OCR reads none of the Greek as Greek.
- Stock, Meno (1887; menostoc00platuoft): the Internet Archive has no OCR text for it.
