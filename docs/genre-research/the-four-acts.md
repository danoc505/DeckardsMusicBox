# THE FOUR ACTS — doom, sludge, mathcore, prog rock across one record

*Researched 2026-08-20.*

> **The owner:** *"We want to incorporate the sound of Doom and Sludge metal in
> the first half of the song, move to Mathcore at the peak and end with
> Progrock."*

> **NOTHING HERE HAS BEEN JUDGED BY EAR.** Standing caveat. The numbers say
> what the machine does, not what it sounds like.

This sheet is the companion to `the-weight-and-the-stop.md`, which already read
**sludge, post-hardcore and mathcore** for transfer. It does not repeat that
work. What it adds is the two genres that sheet never covered — **doom** and
**progressive rock** — and the thing that request actually is: **an arc**.

---

## §0 THE REQUEST IS ALREADY THE ARCHITECTURE

The instruction names four positions in a record. Fantasy synth has exactly
four movements, and the genre's own source comment describes itself as:

> "four microgenres, **the whole thing connected by its motif**" — and
> `tempoArc`, `material`, `energy`, `roles` and `setMachines` "all ask the
> MOVEMENT first now. A leg can therefore declare its own band, its own weight,
> its own instruments, its own chords and its own tempo, which between them is
> what the word *microgenre* actually means here."

So this is not a graft onto a structure that resists it. **It is the structure
being asked to do the job it was built for**, with four named idioms instead of
four described moods.

| movement | bars | measured BPM (seeds 1–3) | **the ask** |
|---|---|---|---|
| setting out | 80 | 79–93 | **doom** |
| into the deep | 96 | 91–97 | **sludge** |
| the fight | 128 | 115–123 | **mathcore** |
| the long way home | 72 | ~70–87 | **prog rock** |

### Why doom goes first and sludge second, rather than both spread evenly

The owner said "Doom and Sludge... in the first half", which is two genres and
two movements, and the sources give an unambiguous ordering:

> Sludge metal **"combines doom metal and hardcore punk"**, whereas traditional
> doom "focuses on slower tempos without the punk-influenced aggression
> characteristic of sludge." [corpus:wikipedia/Doom metal]

Sludge is doom **plus** the aggression that the fight is made of. So doom →
sludge → mathcore is a single continuous line of increasing violence, and
putting doom second would run it backwards. The record already gets *harsher and
more amped up* through those two legs by its own energy table (0.60 → 0.82);
this labels what that escalation is made of.

## §1 DOOM METAL, AS TECHNICAL FACT

Same source family as `the-weight-and-the-stop.md` §1, so the grain is
comparable.

| | |
|---|---|
| tempo | **"Tempos typically sit around 40–80 BPM"** (can rise to mid-tempo for contrast) |
| metre | "Explore **6/8 or 12/8 for a lurching swing**; use **half-time feels to thicken grooves**" |
| harmony | "memorable, minor-key riffs using **Aeolian or Phrygian flavors**, blue notes, **tritones**, and chromatic slides" |
| riff | "Power chords, **parallel fourths/fifths**, and sustained unisons enhance mass"; "**cyclical, mantra-like repetition** with subtle variation" |
| feel | **"Keep drums behind the beat"** with heavy kick/snare emphasis; long sustains |
| dynamics | "space and decay"; **"strategic silences**, feedback swells, and **drone layers**" |
| drums | **"Sparse fills feel enormous at slow tempos"**; "roomy, natural ambience" |
| production | "prioritize **low-mid heft**, natural drum rooms, controlled high-end fizz" |

[corpus:melodigging/doom-metal]

And from the lineage source:

> "the consistent focus on **slow tempos**"; "much use of dissonance
> (**especially in the form of the tritone**)"; "**repetitive rhythms with
> little regard to harmonic progression** and musical structure". Black Sabbath
> "are generally regarded as the progenitors of doom metal".
> [corpus:wikipedia/Doom metal]

### What this genre hands the record for free

Four of doom's eight rows describe things **this program already does or
already has a knob for**, which is why it is a cheap act to write:

- **"cyclical, mantra-like repetition"** and **"drone layers"** — this record's
  native idiom, and `drone` is a role.
- **"repetitive rhythms with little regard to harmonic progression"** — the
  record's measured 7–10 chords in twenty minutes. `the-weight-and-the-stop.md`
  §3.1 argues this only counts as doom if it is **chosen**; the same argument
  applies here and is not restated.
- **"parallel fourths/fifths"** — `parallels: 0.85` is already declared on the
  parent genre for organum. Doom and medieval organum want the identical
  interval, arrived at from opposite ends of eight centuries.
- **"low-mid heft"** — `desk.mid` exists.

The one that is **not** free, and is the act's defining feel:

> **"Keep drums behind the beat."**

## §2 PROGRESSIVE ROCK, AS TECHNICAL FACT

| | |
|---|---|
| metre | "Explore **odd and mixed meters (5/4, 7/8, 9/8, additive rhythms) and polymeter**" |
| harmony | "Draw on **modal interchange, extended chords (maj7, 9, 11)**, and **chromatic voice-leading**" |
| structure | "Write **multi-section suites with contrasting tempos, keys, and textures**"; "long-form compositions"; "multi-part suites" with **"recurring motifs"** |
| instruments | "Core: electric guitar, bass, drums, and versatile keyboards (**Hammond, Mellotron, Moog/ARP, piano**)"; "**flute, sax, strings, or choir** for symphonic breadth" |
| dynamics | "**dynamic arcs that justify extended listening**"; "textural contrasts" |
| rhythm | "Use **tempo modulations, metric feints, and syncopation** to create forward momentum" |

[corpus:melodigging/progressive-rock]

### And this is the strangest finding in the sheet

**Progressive rock is the one genre in either research pass whose *timbre*
transfers.** `the-weight-and-the-stop.md` §2 ran eighteen devices through a
transfer test and every single survivor was a behaviour — "not one of the
survivors is a sound", because a synth record cannot borrow a downtuned guitar.

Prog rock's core instrument list is **Mellotron, Moog, piano, flute, strings,
choir**. This program has a `mellotron`, a `bardWind` flute, `erStrings` at
three registers, a mellotron tape choir, and a rack of analogue synth voices.
The walk home's `setMachines` **already** pins the string/pad shelf and the
recorded pads.

So for one act out of four, the transfer test stops being the binding
constraint. That is not a licence to chase timbre — it is the reason the last
act is the cheapest of the four to make convincing.

**The second free gift is bigger.** "Multi-section suites with contrasting
tempos, keys, and textures" and "recurring motifs" is not a device this record
needs to acquire. **It is a description of what this record already is** — four
movements, each with its own chord set, mode, tempo band and instrument list,
"connected by its motif", with the theme transformed leg by leg (whole,
fragmented, diminished, augmented). The prog act is therefore less "add prog"
than "**stop hiding that the whole record is already built like one**".

## §3 THE TRANSFER TEST, FOR THE TWO NEW GENRES

Applying `the-weight-and-the-stop.md` §2's method rather than restating its
results.

| device | genre | transfers? | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| downtuned guitars, feedback | doom | **no** | timbre; no guitar |
| 40–80 BPM | doom | **yes** | `tempoArc` is a multiplier map |
| **drums behind the beat** | doom | **yes** | a time offset — §4 |
| half-time feels | doom | **yes** | `SLUDGE_FEET` already holds a half-time toll |
| sparse fills, enormous at slow tempo | doom | **yes** | fill density is declared |
| **strategic silences / drone layers** | doom | **yes** | the STOP — a fourth genre naming it |
| mantra repetition | doom | **yes** | native |
| parallel fourths/fifths | doom | **yes** | `parallels` exists |
| tritone as deliberate dissonance | doom | **partly** | the program throws on out-of-key notes by law; needs the voicer to *choose* it, not the transposer to fail. Same caveat as the other sheet's §5 |
| 6/8 or 12/8 | doom | **no, as asked** | a metre build; the other sheet's §4.6 stands |
| **odd/mixed metres 5/4, 7/8, 9/8** | prog | **no, as asked** | same |
| **polymeter** | prog | **yes** | cycle lengths, not signatures — and now **two** genres ask for it |
| **Mellotron, flute, strings, choir** | prog | **yes** | the program owns all four |
| extended chords maj7/9/11 | prog | **yes** | `extensions` already exists |
| modal interchange | prog | **yes** | the movement already changes mode |
| **multi-section suite, recurring motif** | prog | **already built** | §2 |
| tempo modulation as a ratio | prog | **yes** | the other sheet's §4.5, and prog names it too |
| dynamic arcs over long form | prog | **already built** | `form.arc` |

**Doom's defining feel and prog's defining structure both pass.** The two
things that do not are the two metre builds, and they are refused on the same
grounds the previous sheet refused them.

## §4 THE CONVERGENCE, WHICH IS NOW FOUR GENRES DEEP

`the-weight-and-the-stop.md` §4.2 called the **stop** the highest-value device
in the sheet on the grounds that three independently-sourced genres named it.
Doom makes four:

- doom — **"strategic silences"**, "space and decay"
- sludge — "dynamic drops to near-silence"
- post-hardcore — "drop to near-silence before a climactic return"
- mathcore — "strategic negative space: sudden silences and dropouts"

And **behind the beat** is now named by two of the four as a defining feel, not
a colour:

- doom — **"Keep drums behind the beat"**
- sludge — riffs **"drag slightly behind the beat"**

with the contrast supplied by the act between them:

- mathcore — "tight, articulate tones… **fast-transient** drum sounds"

That is the arc in one sentence: **the first half drags, the peak snaps to the
grid, and the drag is what makes the snap land.**

## §5 THE BUILD ORDER

Ordered by what each act needs that it does not have, cheapest and most
defining first.

### 1. THE DRAG, PER MOVEMENT — `setLean`

Doom's and sludge's shared defining feel, and the one thing that will make the
first half *sound* like doom rather than like a fast march.

`laneLean` exists — a fixed per-lane time offset in seconds, built for Hawtin's
machine drift, declared by **lofi and nobody else**. Measured: **every pitched
role carries a lane equal to its role name** (`bass`, `keys`, `keys2`, `lead`,
`ostinato`, `drone`, `counter`), so the mechanism can already address every
part in the band. What it cannot do is **change between movements**, and the
whole request is that it should.

The routing already exists in five other tables — `(sec.mv && X[sec.mv]) ||
X[sec.fn]` is the established idiom, and `sec.mv` is in scope in
`makePerformance`'s section loop.

**Cost: a per-movement lookup on an existing mechanism.**

⚠ The previous sheet's warning stands and is the real risk here: *a lean small
enough to be inaudible is a dead config; one large enough to read as an error is
a bug.* lofi's own comment gives the ceiling — "a full 32nd at this tempo is
about 89 ms, which is further than a whole genre should sit."

#### BUILT, AND IT FOUND A DEAD CONFIG ON THE WAY IN

`laneLean` could not reach a pitched part at all. The lean asked `LEAN[n.lane
|| ""]`; twelve lines below, the event is built as `lane: n.lane || role`. Same
quantity, two fallbacks, one function — and **only drum notes carry `n.lane` on
the material**, so every bass, keys, lead, ostinato, drone and counter note in
the program looked itself up under the empty string.

**lofi's `bass: 0.030` had never moved a note.** Its own comment is entirely
about that entry — "the parts that trail are the hats and the BASS — *the bass
furthest*", reasoned bar by bar from Ethan Hein on Slum Village ("several of
those notes are an entire 32nd note late") and scaled back from 89 ms to 30
[corpus:ethanhein/get-dis-money]. The two hat entries worked. The sourced one
did not.

**And the obvious repair was wrong, which cost a measurement to find out.**
Changing the `lane` variable itself fixes the lookup — and also re-keys
`stream(seed, "thin:…:" + lane)` and the `slideSafe` set for every pitched note
in the file, because `lane` is a **stream key** as much as a name. Measured on
lofi seed 1: keys 484 → 480, lead 110 → 114. A timing repair had quietly become
a composition change. So `lane` is left exactly as it was and only the lean asks
the other question.

Re-measured after the narrow fix, lofi seed 1: **1489 events before, 1489
after; every lane +0.0 ms except bass, which moved +30.0 ms on all 97 of its
notes.** Exactly the declared number, and nothing else.

#### WHAT THE ARC MEASURES

Mean offset from the grid, per lane, fantasy synth, seeds 1–3:

| lane | setting out (doom) | into the deep (sludge) | the fight (mathcore) |
|---|---|---|---|
| kick | **+34.0** | +7.7 | +7.7 |
| snare | **+47.3** | +8.2 | +7.5 |
| toms | **+34 … +41** | +6.0 … +8.2 | +6.8 … +8.8 |
| bass | **+29.4** | **+33.7** | +1.4 |
| keys / keys2 | **+27.5 / +22.4** | **— / +31.9** | +0.2 / +0.3 |
| lead | −0.5 | +16.5 | +0.5 |

(+7.7 ms on the drums is the genre's existing `push`, not a lean — it is the
floor these numbers sit on.)

The three acts read as their sources describe them. **Doom**: the whole floor
sags together, snare furthest back. **Sludge**: the kit returns to the line —
no drum lane is named in that leg at all — and the weight drags against it, the
tension doom does not have. **Mathcore**: everything snaps to the grid.

Synthwave and dungeon synth declare neither `laneLean` nor `setLean` and are
**byte-identical**; 20 genre/seed runs clean.

### 2. THE TEMPO BANDS, CHOSEN FROM SOURCES INSTEAD OF FEEL

The arc's multipliers were `[CHOSEN]` as feel. Two of the four acts now come
with a stated band, so those two stop being taste:

| act | sourced band | measured now | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| doom | **40–80** | 79–93 | **above it** — the top edge only just touches |
| sludge | 60–110 | 91–97 | inside |
| mathcore | — none stated | 115–123 | — |
| prog | — none stated | ~70–87 | — |

Only doom's leg actually needs to move, and `tempoArc` is normalised, so
slowing it **speeds everything else up** by the same total. That is free
contrast in the direction the request wants.

### 3. POLYMETER AT THE PEAK AND AT THE END

Asked for by mathcore *and* by prog, independently, in the same words:
"polymeter". The previous sheet's §4.4 designs it — one lane on a cycle coprime
with the bar — and its honest caveat carries over unchanged: **this is the safe
half of the gist.** 7-against-4 gives the disorientation without the metric
shift, and calling it "mathcore" or "prog" rather than "polymeter" would be
overselling it.

### 4. THE STOP

Four genres, one device, and the record's worst measured fault. Unchanged from
the previous sheet's §7 except that it is now better supported.

## §6 WHAT I AM NOT PROPOSING

- **Not four different genres of record.** The genre's own comment is the
  guardrail: what connects the legs "is unchanged and is the point: one motif,
  transformed leg by leg… one story." Four acts, one record.
- **Not odd metres**, for either doom or prog. Refused on the previous sheet's
  §4.6 grounds, not re-litigated.
- **Not distortion.** Doom's own production line says **"low-mid heft"** before
  it says anything about gain.
- **Not renaming the movements.** "Setting out", "into the deep", "the fight"
  and "the long way home" are the record's story. Doom, sludge, mathcore and
  prog are how those four are *played*, which is the same relationship the two
  foot-vocabularies already have to the march.
- **Not dissonance as licence** — the tritone caveat in §3 is real and the
  out-of-key law is a guardrail worth keeping.

## §7 OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE OWNER

1. **How slow does the first act go?** Doom's band bottoms at 40. Taking
   "setting out" to the middle of it (≈60) is a very different record from
   nudging it to the top edge (≈78). The arc is normalised, so whatever comes
   off this leg goes onto the fight.
2. **Does the walk home drag or resolve?** Carried forward unanswered from the
   previous sheet's §8.3, and prog sharpens it: a prog ending is usually a
   *resolution*, which argues for the grid rather than the drag.
3. **Is prog's timbre licence wanted?** §2 says the last act is the one where
   this program can legitimately chase the sound and not just the behaviour.
   That is a bigger aesthetic step than anything else here.
