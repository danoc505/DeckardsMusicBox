# The Rhodes, and why the tune is not playing one

*Researched 2026-08-04. The user: "We have a Rhodes if that is named as
something used why are we not improving it and using it for lead? if you have
gathered data on lofi why are we not implementing it?" Both halves are correct
and the previous session's "needs research first" was wrong on both — the
instrument is here, the sources naming it are already gathered, and the only
thing missing was the wire between them. This sheet researches the INSTRUMENT,
which had never been done, and measures what ours already does.*

---

## 1. WHAT A RHODES IS, PHYSICALLY

Not a piano with a pickup. Each key drives a hammer at a **tine** — a stiff
steel rod — bolted beside a **tone bar**, a tuned resonator. The tine vibrates,
the tone bar sustains it, and an **electromagnetic pickup** a few millimetres
away turns that motion into a voltage.

> Each note is "a dedicated assembly where a hammer strikes a flexible steel
> tine, initiating oscillations that are **sympathetically reinforced by an
> attached tone bar**, creating a rich harmonic spectrum."
> [corpus:grokipedia Rhodes piano; corpus:wikipedia Rhodes piano]

**The tone is set by geometry, not by a filter.** Where the tine sits relative
to the pickup decides the harmonic balance:

> "As the tip of the tine gets closer to the pickup axis, **the fundamental and
> odd harmonics decrease**, causing the second harmonic to rise as the strongest
> frequency of the spectrum." The tine and pickup "behave **nonlinearly**, which
> adds harmonic distortion to the original signal." Off-axis settings give "a
> rounded, mellower tone", on-axis "more mid-range with less low end", and
> putting tine and pickup close together gives "**a characteristic bell
> sound**." [corpus:shear, UCSB masters thesis]

**`[three sources]`**

## 2. THE BARK, AND IT IS A DYNAMIC — the finding that matters here

> "The '**bark**' is produced by the overtones generated by the Rhodes' tine and
> tone bar when proper escapement and pickup distance is set to capture these
> overtones within their magnetic fields… **when setup properly, the dynamic
> range of the bark can be set such that the player can bring out more or less
> bark with more or less forte in their playing.**"
> [corpus:chicagoelectricpiano]

That sentence is the whole design brief for this instrument in software. **The
bark is not a tone control. It is what happens when you hit it harder** — and
therefore it is a property of the NOTE, not of the patch.

Our `V.rhodes` already understands this and says so in its own comment: the
`bark` knob "biases which velocity LAYER the sampler reaches for… it is not a
filter". The bank holds soft, mid and hard recordings of every note. That is
the correct model, arrived at before this research existed.

---

## 3. WHAT OURS ACTUALLY IS — better than the complaint implied

`V.rhodes` is **a sampler, not a synth approximation**. It reads a real
electric-piano bank (`g.epiano`), picks a root by register, picks one of three
velocity layers by how hard the note is struck, loops the sustain, and decays
on the source's own law — `tau = e^(2 − 0.03·note)` seconds, note clamped at 44,
with a separate release when the key lifts. It carries the tape wow and flutter
on `playbackRate`, which is where pitch instability physically belongs.

**So the instrument was never the problem.** The problem is that nothing points
the tune at it.

---

## 4. TWO THINGS MEASURED, AND ONE OF THEM IS A REAL DEFECT

### 4a. Nothing plays the Rhodes' hard layer, and the comp can't reach it

Which of the three velocity layers each note lands in, computed exactly as
`V.rhodes` computes it, lofi, 60 seeds:

```
  the comp   soft 32.3%   mid 67.7%   hard  0.0%     (34 969 notes)
  the tune   soft  0.1%   mid 94.9%   hard  5.0%     (11 554 notes)
```

**Zero of 34 969.** The bark — the thing §2 calls the instrument's signature and
the thing our own sampler was built with three layers to deliver — is
unreachable by the part that plays three quarters of the Rhodes notes in this
program.

**And the cause is arithmetic, not taste.** The layer switches at velocity 80.
The velocity is `gain × 100 × (0.72 + 0.56 × bark)`, lofi declares
`bark: 0.42`, and lofi's comp gains top out around 0.79 — so the hardest chord
in the genre arrives at **75**, five short, every time. Nothing in the genre
table says "never bark"; it falls out of two numbers that were set
independently.

**STATED HONESTLY: this is not obviously a bug to FIX by raising a number.**
A comping left hand genuinely is played softer than a melody, and the sources
agree the bark is what forte does. What is wrong is that the ceiling is
*accidental* — 5 velocity points below a threshold nobody was thinking about
when they set `bark: 0.42`. Whether lofi's comp should bark at all is an a verdict on it
question. **That the tune should is not**, and the tune is not playing a Rhodes
at all, which is §5.

### 4b. The tune must NOT get the wobble the comp gets

> "**A background Rhodes can handle substantial flutter** because the notes
> blend together anyway, but **the main melody needs to stay steady enough to
> be recognizable.**" [corpus:northernvalleyaudio]

Checked before building anything: `ev.wow` and `ev.flutter` are set for
`role === "keys" || role === "keys2"` and for nothing else. **So a Rhodes
handed the lead already gets no drift, and the sourced rule is satisfied by
code that predates the source.** Recorded rather than built — the temptation
was to add a "lead gets less wow" branch that would have been dead.

## 5. WHAT THE SOURCES SAY SHOULD PLAY THE TUNE — already gathered, restated

From `lofi-comp-and-lead.md` §5, three sources, none of which names a synth:

> "**Rhodes piano is the standard choice.** Vibraphone works well at lower
> tempos. A muted guitar played fingerstyle sits naturally in the mix without
> competing with the chords. **Pick one melodic instrument and keep it in its
> mid-range.**" [corpus:songer]

> Electric pianos are "one of the most iconic instruments in lo-fi music", with
> a "dreamy and mellow timbre"; Fender Rhodes and Wurlitzer especially.
> Synthesisers are listed for a different job — "**soft pads, warm chords, and
> gentle arpeggios**". [corpus:clarkaudio]

> "Rhodes keyboards and analog synths", with the synth **layered under** a real
> instrument. [corpus:nativeinstruments]

And what it should be run through:

> "To give your electric piano a classic feel, consider effects like **tremolo
> or chorus**… a Fender Rhodes with tremolo creates a gentle pulsating sound
> that gives the melody a vintage vibe." [corpus:mysticalankar Lead Instruments]

This program already has that tremolo — on the Wurlitzer, built and calibrated
after a verdict report that it pulsed too hard.

## 6. WHAT WAS BUILT — `2026-08-04i`

**`leadChar`, beside `keysChar`.** A genre says what character its tune has, the
same way it already says what character its comp has. Every genre declares one;
six declare `"synth"`, which is the voice they already had, so they are
byte-identical and sound identical. lofi declares `[["rhodes", 6], ["wurly", 4]]`
— **the same pair it already names for its comp**, because the sources name that
pair and this genre already owns both instruments.

**Drawn on its own substream.** `wpick(stream(seed, "leadchar"), G.leadChar)`
rather than another `rng()` inside `makeChart` — a new draw on the chart's own
stream would have shifted every draw after it and moved every note in all seven
genres, a change to the record hidden inside a change to the band. A named
substream is what Law 3 is for and this is the case it was written for.

**THE RIG STILL DECIDES WHO IS IN THE ROOM.** The dispatch lives inside
`V.lead`, which is the voice the `band` rig points its lead lane at. A lofi song
drawn on the `sega` rig still plays a chip lead, because on that rig the lane
never reaches `V.lead` at all. A rig is a band; `leadChar` is what that band's
lead player is holding.

**`V.rhodes` and `V.wurly` now ask the note which channel it belongs to** rather
than assuming the keyboard one. Both were hard-wired to the `keys` bus and
`LEVEL.keys`; a Rhodes playing the tune has to arrive on the lead channel or the
mixer is lying — the fader marked "lead" would move nothing and the fader marked
"keys" would move the tune.

**And the level was measured, not guessed — with an A/B inside ONE build.**
Stashing the change and rendering the old file compares two commits; forcing
`ev.timbre` to `"synth"` on a second pass over the SAME events compares two
INSTRUMENTS, which is the question. lofi, band-rig seeds, lead and counter
events only:

```
  seed  1  wurly    rms 0.595x   peak 0.745x
  seed  2  rhodes   rms 0.996x   peak 0.960x
  seed  5  wurly    rms 0.847x   peak 0.840x
  seed  7  rhodes   rms 0.728x   peak 0.714x

  mean   0.79x on RMS,  0.82x on peak  —  about 2 dB QUIETER
```

**The arithmetic predicted the opposite.** The bank peaks at 0.74 and is scaled
up by 2.4; the house lead's oscillator stack peaks near 1.68 and is divided back
down by it, which says the Rhodes should arrive HOTTER. It arrives quieter,
because an electric piano note decays and a held oscillator does not. That is
the whole reason this is rendered rather than reasoned about, and it is this
project's own standing rule — when a measurement surprises you, suspect the
measurement first, then suspect the reasoning.

`LEAD_KBD_TRIM = 1.25` puts it back. **One number for both instruments**: the
Rhodes reads −1.4 dB and the Wurlitzer −2.4 dB, and with two seeds each that
difference is not something this data separates. Two constants would be a
precision the measurement does not have.

**What it does NOT touch:** the trim applies only where a keyboard stands in for
the house lead, so every comp in every genre renders exactly what it did.

### And what the notes did — nothing

```
  the snapshot          1500 of 2100 seeds differ
  with `timbre` stripped   IDENTICAL, 2100 seeds
```

The snapshot hashes **every field an event carries**, on purpose — three
separate times this project shipped a change a hand-written field list could not
see. So a new field changes it by construction and "1500 differ" is not evidence
about the notes either way. The 1500 are exactly the five genres that write a
lead; plastikman and jungle write none and their 600 are untouched. Stripping
the new field and re-hashing gives a byte-identical answer: **not one note
moved.** The baseline was then re-written deliberately.

### And which songs actually changed instrument

```
  lofi, 300 seeds:   band/rhodes 136   band/wurly 74   sega/rhodes 55   sega/wurly 35
```

**210 of 300.** The 90 on the `sega` rig still play a chip lead, because on that
rig the lead lane resolves to `chipLead` and never reaches `V.lead` at all. The
`leadChar` they drew is a dead draw, exactly like the `keysChar` those genres
have always carried — spent so the stream stays aligned, read by nobody.

## 7. WHAT THE SOURCES DO NOT SETTLE

- **Whether the comp should bark.** §4a is a measurement, not a verdict. The
  sources say forte brings the bark out; they do not say how hard a lofi comper
  plays. Left alone deliberately.
- **Vibraphone and muted guitar.** Two of the three named lead instruments, and
  this program has neither. `V.mellotron` and the sampled bank are the nearest
  things and neither is one. Not built, not faked.
- **How a tube amp's contribution should be modelled.** [corpus:chicagoelectric]
  says Rhodes players use tube amps to bring out the overtones; nothing here
  measures what that does or whether the sampled bank already contains it.
