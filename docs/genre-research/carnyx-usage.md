# How a carnyx was actually used — the usage sheet the voice never had

*Researched 2026-08-15. The owner: "can we make sure we are using the carnyx
properly?" The voice itself (`V.carnyx`) was built from the Tintignac and
Deskford reconstruction measurements and is physically honest. What was never
researched is the USAGE — what kind of part an Iron Age war horn plays. This
sheet is that.*

## 1. It is a signal instrument

The carnyx coordinated assaults. "The loud sounds produced by the instruments
would **signal the start of swift, violent assaults, where timing was
critical**" [twz.com, "The Carnyx's Eerie Cry"]. It speaks at moments — the
charge, the arrival, the apex — not continuously.

Diodorus Siculus, on its character: "They blow into them and produce a **harsh
sound which suits the tumult of war**."

## 2. When massed, it is massed in noise, not in harmony

Polybius on Telamon (225 BC): "There were **countless trumpeters and horn
blowers** and since the whole army was shouting its war cries at the same time
there was such a confused sound that the noise seemed to come not only from
the trumpeters and the soldiers but also from the countryside which was
joining in the echo."

Many carnyces at once is historical — but as a *wall of independent blasts*,
not as chord voicing. Nobody dealt a triad across a Gallic horn line.

## 3. What the reconstructions added

John Kenny — the first person in two thousand years to play a restored carnyx
(1993, the Deskford instrument, with National Museums Scotland) — showed the
instrument is "capable of subtle musicality, not just noise": rich brassy
**drones**, shifts to a high **scream**, growls, and articulated calls. The
Tintignac replica work (European Music Archaeology Project, bronze by Jean
Boisserie) confirmed "a greater and more subtle range of sound" than the
battle accounts alone suggest — and that playing it fully upright is nearly
impossible; players hold it at an angle. [twz.com; archaeology.org,
"Sonic Warfare of the Celts"; instrumentheritage.com]

So the honest palette is: **the blast** (signal, loud, timed), **the drone**
(one sustained pitch, the tube's own resonance), **the scream/growl** (apex
colour). All of it is ONE VOICE AT A TIME from one instrument — the carnyx is
a lip-reed tube with no valves; it plays one note, chosen from its handful of
resonances.

## 4. What this program was doing, measured

- Dungeon synth pooled the carnyx on **keys2 — the sustained chord pad**
  (`["carnyx", 2]`): block chords, held for bars, at whatever energy the
  section happens to have. Two improprieties at once: a monophonic signal
  instrument voicing CHORDS, and a war horn PADDING quietly through verses.
- The pad band the pool grants it is [62,77]; its measured resonances put
  real support near MIDI ≈60, 64.5, 72, 75, 77 — so a pad chord spread across
  that band lands half its tones in the support hole (65–71), where the
  voice's own physics makes them speak thin. The voice was being honest; the
  part was wrong.
- Hobbit synth's keys2 ladder already gets the ENERGY right — carnyx on the
  top rung, "only sounds when the record is at its loudest" — but the rung is
  still a chord pad. That half stays open with task #58 (dealing a chord
  across monophonic voices), and is recorded here rather than half-fixed.

## 5. What is built from this sheet

Dungeon synth's carnyx moves from the chord pad to **the answering line** —
the counter, a single derived line that speaks in the gaps of the tune
(`deriveCounter`), played by the bastion rig's brass section when the genre
stages it. A war horn low-weighted into that pool is the signal instrument in
its actual role: **one voice, at moments, answering** — and the existing
apex-riding of `blaze` (built when the voice was) finally has a part shaped
like the thing it rides. The keys2 pad pool entry is removed in the same
breath. Blast radius: dungeon synth only, and the sheet says so before the
snapshot does.
