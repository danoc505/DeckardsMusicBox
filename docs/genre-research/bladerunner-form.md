## The method — and why it matters to a generative program

Vangelis filled the studio with video monitors and **improvised to picture,
live, with no rehearsal**: he would *"create a composition straight away"*
when the images connected, and *"if viewing the film scene induced no
immediate reaction [he] would not push himself to force the music out."*
He *"always prefers to use his first take... even if the recording contains
small mistakes, because he sees his first takes as more honest."* Overdubs
were kept *"to a bare minimum, to avoid turning his music sick by tampering
with it beyond what was necessary."* His own summary: the score *"is not on
paper but on tape."*

For this program the lesson is a constraint, not a mechanism: the cues are
**through-composed performances, not built arrangements**. Nothing repeats
on a grid; the shape is a single arc of intensity — the Nemo account of the
love theme: *"The music changes in intensity as it progresses, from being
apprehensive and doubtful to confident."*

## The shape of the cues

- **Main Titles is TWO-PART with a late arrival.** *"It's played very
  rubato"* — a long, free, atmospheric first part — and the second part
  *"picks up around 2:23"* [Jamieson]. The theme arrives well past halfway.
- **Harmonic stasis is a legitimate whole-cue form.** Blade Runner Blues is
  *"quite a simple CS-80 improvisation on just two chords, F#m and D"* —
  timbral development carries the piece, not harmonic rhythm [Jamieson].
- **The departure is a REHARMONIZATION, not new material.** Blush Response
  shifts the main motif *"from the original key of E major to D Phrygian"*
  [Jamieson] — the same idea in stranger light, which is what this
  program's C-material bridge already is.
- **Mostly beatless.** *"The absence of drums (as with much of the score)"*
  [Jamieson]. The genre's nemo rig already has no kit; the research
  confirms rather than changes it.
- **One cue drives.** End Titles: sequenced bass, 113 bpm, 4:39 — the
  exception that stays an exception.

## The plan (as landed in `GENRE.bladerunner.form.plan`)

    drift:     verse/instrumental, 16-24 bars — the rubato bed; the theme
               has not arrived yet
    statement: chorus/verse, 8-16 bars, ends on chorus — the arrival,
               "picks up" late in the cue
    away:      bridge/instrumental/verse, 8-16 bars — the reharmonized
               departure (Blush Response's move)
    return:    chorus/verse, 8-16 bars, ends on chorus — "apprehensive and
               doubtful to confident": the last statement is the biggest

Four phases expressing one documented arc. With intro and outro outside the
plan, totals land in the 60-90 bar range at this genre's slow tempos —
several minutes a cue, in the score's own territory. Budgets [EAR] within
the sourced shape.

## Noted for LATER, deliberately not acted on here

- **Two-chord stasis** (Blade Runner Blues) is a HARMONY finding, not a
  form one: a `progressions` entry with two chords, and this genre would
  have the Blues' bed to improvise over. It belongs to the harmony work
  (chromaticism, §5 of the handoff), where it should be done with the
  voice-leading tools, not smuggled in as a form rider.
- **Rubato** — the first parts are played FREE. This program has no
  mechanism for tempo that breathes inside a section (groove jitter is not
  rubato). A real gap, honestly larger than a table entry; written here so
  it stops being invisible.
