## §1 THE IDEA, and everything else here follows from it

**A techno track is not a song, and our program only knows how to write songs.**

That is the whole finding. Every specific thing the owner noticed is a symptom
of it:

> *"it feels like its almost all percussion sounds and some fx sounds like a
> vocal sample or a sound. If there was bass it was supporting the bass drum…
> I bet you could make techno with two drum machines and fx, i believe someone
> found that the melodie was coming from a drum. And the low mid highs being cut
> and brought up is vital."*

Read together rather than one at a time, those five observations say: **there is
no melodic layer at all.** No tune, no chords, no bass part. The music is made of
**timbre and frequency moving over time**, and the notes — such as they are —
come out of the drum machine. Everything a song does with melody and harmony,
techno does with tone and filtering.

Our pipeline is a song machine: a key and a chord progression, then verses and
choruses, then a bass line and a keyboard and a lead over the chords. You cannot
reach techno by turning that machine's dials down. **Techno is a different shape
of object, and the honest answer to "everything else is wrong" is that the
architecture is what is wrong, not the numbers in the table.**

## §2 THE SOURCES, and what each one settles

*Fetched this session unless marked search-level.*

### It is a TOOL, not a piece — and that decides the structure

> "A techno track is functional music designed to be played inside a two-hour DJ
> set, so the structure has to serve the blend, not just the home listen."
> [tracksensei]

> "Its main purpose is to be mixed with other tracks, to enter, coexist, and exit
> smoothly within a continuous flow of music… Intros and outros are functional,
> not expressive." [audioservices/pheek, search-level]

> "Phrase everything in 16 and 32 bar blocks, because DJs count in phrases and a
> 24-bar section throws off every mix that touches it." [tracksensei]

**A verse is a claim about meaning. A 32-bar block is a claim about mixing.** Our
form grammar is built out of the first kind and techno is built out of the
second.

### It is MADE BY PLAYING A MACHINE, and the record is a document of that

Derrick May, in his own words, on the Belleville Three's method:

> **"We were playing the music. We weren't using the synthesizers or the
> sequencers or the programs just as a sort of crutch."** [rbma lecture]

> **"I don't want a computer to tell me what I can and can't do."** [rbma lecture]

Jeff Mills on the TR-909: the way it was built "makes it possible to really play
the machine, not just program it… because of the stop/start function, you can
manipulate it as if you're playing a sample, and if you're really good, almost as
if you're a live drummer playing a drum kit" [mixmag, search-level]. He performs
with "three decks, a Roland 909 drum-machine and seventy records in one hour"
[gearnews/mixmag, search-level] — **the 909 is not a backing track, it is the
instrument.**

And the technique tutorials describe the same thing as a production method:
"Assign the open hat's Release to a MIDI controller… repeat this for the
Transpose", then **"lay down some live Transpose and Release tweaks over 16
bars"** [musicradar, Mills-style 909].

**That is the answer to "the melody was coming from a drum", and it is bigger
than tuned toms.** The performance IS the transposing and the opening of a drum
voice. The pitch material of a techno record can be a drum machine's tune knob
being turned.

### THE OWNER'S "TWO DRUM MACHINES AND FX" IS LITERALLY THE RECORD

Robert Hood's *Minimal Nation*, the record that names the whole idea, was made
with pawn-shop gear: a 909, an SH-101, a Juno 2, a borrowed DX100, a Yamaha QY20
and **"a $50 four-track mixer"** [rbma / djmag]. And the method:

> "The formula starts with one basic synth riff, adds a pounding kick drum, then
> **modulates, tweaks and filters** the melody while **adding and subtracting
> layers of percussion**. Typically, there were **not more than four elements at
> any point** in the song." [djmag]

> "Motifs were often **only a single bar**, percussion could be as simple as kick
> drum and a hi-hat, and the spaces in between the notes were elevated to the
> same importance as the notes themselves." [djmag]

Hood himself: **"rhythms inside of rhythms inside of rhythms — sort of hidden
rhythms"**, and **"Take your time, and get into a groove, and get into a rhythm,
and stick to it."** [rbma track-by-track]

**A one-bar motif, four elements, and everything that happens after that is
filtering and adding/removing percussion.** That is the owner's "two drum
machines and fx" stated by the man who made the record.

### THERE IS NO MELODIC INSTRUMENT — a full worked example says so outright

Attack Magazine's step-by-step build of a dark techno groove uses: a 909 kick, a
second kick with long decay for the rumble, a shaker, a short open hat, a closed
hat, a long open hat, and a ride. The verdict at the end of the breakdown:

> **"No melodic instruments are used. The beat consists entirely of percussion
> and bass elements."** [attack, Dark Techno Rumble]

### THE BASS IS THE KICK — the owner's observation, confirmed exactly

> "The concept involves making a booming sub bass that is **in the same key as
> the kick, as it is made directly from the kick itself**… Because the kick and
> sub bass come from the same sound source, they have similar tonal qualities,
> and **the bass feels like an extension of the kick**… This technique… allows
> you to create powerful low-end rumble **without needing a separate bassline**."
> [mastrng / studiobrootle, search-level; the Attack build does the same thing
> with "reverb with long decay… plus a low pass filter"]

Numbers from the Attack build: the main kick rolled off "until approximately
50Hz" with "a dip of 3dB around 500Hz"; the rumble layer is a second kick with
long reverb, low-passed, "drive knob to just under 4dB".

**So "if there was bass it was supporting the bass drum" is not a mixing
observation. It is a statement about what the bass IS: the kick's own tail.**

### AND THE FREQUENCY BANDS ARE PLAYED LIKE AN INSTRUMENT

The owner: *"the low mid highs being cut and brought up is vital."* This is not
EQ-as-mixing. It is EQ-as-composition, and it has a name and a lineage:

> Derrick May "uses his isolator to create a jagged sound sculpture with abrupt
> moments spent on each band", functioning as **"an entirely rhythmic tool"**
> rather than for EQ purposes. [djtechtools]

> "In contrast to traditional EQs, isolators are generally not used for the
> purposes of mixing, but instead to shape the sound and add color… Isolators
> allow you to create drama during a DJ performance." An isolator "can completely
> kill the high, mid or low frequencies", with "much more gain per band
> (sometimes double)" and a far wider band than a mixer EQ — the example given is
> 10–300 Hz for the low band against a DJM-800's 10–100 Hz. [djtechtools]

And in production the same move is the arrangement itself: **"automate it
deliberately over 32 or 64 bars"** [myloops], "filter or even apply reverb to the
entire stereo mix in order to create more dramatic moments of tension and
release" [musicradar].

**A techno arrangement is largely: which frequency bands are present right now.**
Taking the lows out and putting them back is the equivalent of a chorus arriving.

## §3 WHAT OUR ARCHITECTURE ASSUMES, STAGE BY STAGE, AND WHAT TECHNO SAYS TO IT

This is the part the first sheet missed.

| stage | what it assumes | what techno says |
|---|---|---|
| **1 chart** | every record has a KEY, a MODE and a four-chord progression, plus bridge chords | there is no progression. One pedal tone, or no tonal centre a listener could name. Every part downstream reads a chord bed that should not exist |
| **2 form** | a song is a grammar over verse / chorus / bridge / pre-chorus, with a PAYOFF the record returns to and a RULE OF THREE forcing variation on the third hearing | there are 16- and 32-bar blocks, and at each boundary one element enters or leaves. There is no payoff and no third-time change — Hood says *stick to it* |
| **3 materials** | the music is notes, written for roles — bass, keys, keys2, lead, counter, ostinato — voiced over the chords | there are no melodic roles. Percussion, and at most one sample or stab used as texture. The pitch material comes out of the drum machine |
| **4 arrangement** | which ROLES play in this section | which FREQUENCY BANDS and which percussion layers are present in this block |
| **5 performance** | groove, velocity, micro-timing applied to notes | correct as-is, and already good — the swing and lane-lean numbers match the sources |
| **6 sound** | voices, the graph, the effects | correct as-is, and the owner has said so twice: the drum reverb and echo stay |

**The rule of three is the sharpest case.** It is a hard law of this program —
"state it, confirm it, change it on the third" — and it is a law of SONGS. Techno
is the music that refuses it. `form.varyAsBefore` and `form.vary: false` exist to
soften it, but softening a law is not the same as a genre that is built on its
opposite.

## §4 THE PROGRAM ALREADY HAS MOST OF WHAT TECHNO NEEDS — it just is not pointed there

This is the good news and it is why this is worth doing.

| what techno needs | what already exists |
|---|---|
| pitch coming out of a drum voice | **`chTune` reads a per-event machine parameter through the p-lock system** (`<group>Tune`). A drum lane's tuning can already be sequenced step by step — `parameter-locks.md` records jungle locking the pitch of break slices. **This is "the melody comes from a drum", already buildable** |
| polymetric percussion, "rhythms inside of rhythms" | `kit.poly` — plastikman already runs a 7-step rim and a 5-step clap against a 16-step bar |
| a lane that answers another lane | `kit.listen` |
| a sample used as texture | the break machine / `chop`, currently jungle's |
| frequency bands cut and restored as the arrangement | the matrix mixer, the sends, the filters and the whole motion system |
| slow unsynced tonal drift | the motion system; p-locks |
| a genre owning its own architecture instead of the song grammar | **`form.plan`** — an ordered spine of phases with pools and bar budgets. Five genres already use one. **This is where 16/32-bar blocks with one element entering per block would live** |
| the kick and its tail as the bass | the kick voicing, the room and the ducking all exist |

**Almost nothing new has to be invented.** What is missing is a genre that
switches the song machinery OFF and leans entirely on the machinery above.

## §5 WHAT IS GENUINELY MISSING

1. **A way for a genre to say "I have no harmony".** Stage 1 always draws a
   progression and stage 3 always voices to it. Techno needs a pedal-tone mode
   where the chord bed is one chord for the whole record and nothing reads it as
   movement.
2. **A way for a genre to say "I have no melodic roles".** Setting `form.roles`
   to drums-only silences everything, which is not the same as having the pitch
   material live in the drum machine.
3. **A tune lane** — a sequence of tunings for a drum group, drawn like a
   material, so a tom or a cowbell carries the record's only pitch movement.
   The reader (`chTune`) exists; nothing writes to it as a pattern.
4. **A block-based form** rather than a song grammar: 16/32-bar blocks, one
   element in or out per boundary, no payoff, no rule of three.
5. **Band presence as an arrangement dimension** — the record deciding "no lows
   for these 16 bars" the way it currently decides "no keys in this section".
6. **The kick drop** — the one move techno owns that we have nothing like, and it
   is a HARD LAW being broken on purpose (the drum arc protects the kick on every
   strong beat), which is exactly the mechanism `breaking-the-rule.md` §3 called
   for.

## §6 WHAT "MOVING THE GENRE TOWARDS TECHNO" WOULD MEAN

The owner said *"we are moving the genre towards techno"* — a direction for
`plastikman`, not necessarily a ninth genre. Taking that literally, in the order
the sources justify:

1. **Kill the harmony.** One pedal tone. No progression, no bridge chords.
2. **Kill the comping keyboard.** 835 notes a song of fresh voicings is the
   single most un-techno thing in the file.
3. **Make the bass the kick's tail** rather than a separate part.
4. **Give the pitch material to a drum voice** through a tune lane.
5. **Replace the song form with 16/32-bar blocks**, one element per boundary.
6. **Make band presence the arrangement** — lows out, lows back.

**1–3 are subtractions and could be done tomorrow. 4–6 are the real build.**

**And one honest warning, because it is the shape of this project's worst
mistakes:** every item above makes the music SIMPLER, and this program's seam
checks are largely built to catch music being too static — the rule of three, the
"the genres are actually different music" check, the arrival and phrasing checks.
A genuine techno genre will trip several of them, and **the right response is to
let the genre declare that those laws do not apply to it, not to quietly loosen
the law for everybody.** That distinction is the whole of principle 2.

**Nothing here is built. This is research.**
