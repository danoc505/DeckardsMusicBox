# DS2 — putting the metal back into dungeon synth

*Researched 2026-08-27, at the owner's request: "we are trying to make a new
Dungeon Synth genre labeled DS2 that is meant to bring the metal back into
dungeon synth with the heavy distortion and double kick pedals."*

> **NOTHING HERE HAS BEEN JUDGED BY EAR.** Standing caveat. Every number below
> is either a quoted source or a measurement off the printout. None of it is a
> claim that the record sounds good.

This sheet does **not** repeat work already on the shelf. `dungeon-synth.md`
holds the genre's founding research; `the-pedals-are-the-genre.md` holds the
fuzz-circuit sourcing; `the-four-acts.md` and `what-the-fight-is-missing.md`
hold the doom / sludge / hardcore reading done for the deleted `doomsludge`.
What is new here is the thing none of those asked: **what exactly was the metal
that dungeon synth removed, and what do the sources say about putting it back.**

---

## 1. THE GENRE IS DEFINED BY A SUBTRACTION, AND THE SOURCES NAME THE PARTS

This is the single most useful finding for DS2, because it turns "add metal"
into a list of three named items rather than a mood.

> Dungeon synth "employs aesthetics and themes typically associated with black
> metal **juxtaposed to the typical heavy tremolo-picking, blast-beats, and
> harsh, shrieked vocals of black metal** by way of compositions of instrumental
> or ambient music commonly used as introductions, interludes, or outros in
> black metal … albums." [corpus:wikipedia/Dungeon_synth]

So the genre is black metal **minus** exactly:

| removed | DS2's position |
|---|---|
| tremolo picking | **not built.** See §6 — this is the largest gap. |
| blast beats | **partly built** — a 16th double-kick run, no blast. §4. |
| shrieked vocals | out of scope; this program has no voice. |

And the genre is a **derivative of black metal and dark ambient**, emerging in
early-1990s Norway among black metal musicians — Mortiis (ex-Emperor) and Burzum
are the named ancestors, with Mortiis coining "dark dungeon music".
[corpus:wikipedia/Dungeon_synth, corpus:note.com/soundwitches]

**DS2 is therefore not a fusion of two distant things.** It is the parent genre
with its own removed parts put back. That is worth stating in the table's banner,
because it is a much stronger design argument than "dungeon synth with rock in
it" and it names what to build.

## 2. THE PRIOR ART — THREE HYBRIDS THAT ALREADY EXIST

Researching this properly turned up that **the hybrid is not new**, which is
useful in both directions: the moves are known, and DS2 needs to say what it is
doing that the existing ones do not.

### 2a. Summoning — the closest thing to DS2 that exists

Austrian, from 1993, and the canonical "dungeon synth and metal at once".
Wikipedia lists them beside Mortiis, Burzum and Old Tower as notable dungeon
synth artists while they are a metal band. [corpus:wikipedia/Dungeon_synth]

**Their method is directly relevant to this program, because it is this
program's method.** From the band's own interviews:

- They **composed all their songs on keyboards**, which was "the most prominent
  instrument in their sound", and they **used keyboard drums**.
- They "made them sound **as slow and bombastic as possible**."
- Protector "composed cool **guitar riffs on the keyboard**, because playing on
  keys gave him more ideas than playing on strings."
  [corpus:metalstorm/Summoning biography, corpus:distortedsoundmag interview]

Three things follow for DS2 and they are the most transferable findings on this
sheet:

1. **A programmed kit is idiomatic here, not a compromise.** *Minas Morgul* is
   praised for "one of the most competent uses of a drum machine" and criticised
   in the same breath for a "Kraftwerk-esque drum sound"
   [corpus:metal-archives/Minas Morgul reviews]. This program has nothing but a
   programmed kit, and in this one genre that is the correct instrument.
2. **Slow and bombastic is a stated aim, not a limitation.** DS2's quiet legs
   run at 54–61 BPM (§6). That is *on* the idiom, not short of it.
3. **The riff is a keyboard part.** DS2 already writes its riff on the comp and
   the ostinato and drives them through a fuzz. That is Summoning's own
   arrangement, arrived at independently.

### 2b. Tänzelcore / Keller synth — the hybrid taken the other way

Named on Wikipedia as a dungeon synth subgenre that "combines electronic dance
music with extreme metal aesthetics, featuring **high-tempo beats, gabber kicks,
and hardcore techno structures** layered with crude, lo-fi synthesizer melodies."
[corpus:wikipedia/Dungeon_synth]

Worth knowing exists, and worth **not** being: DS2's heavy leg is a metal
climax, not a gabber one. This is the nearest neighbour DS2 has to distinguish
itself from, and the distinguishing feature is that DS2's heavy section is
*inside a dungeon synth record's arc* rather than being the record.

### 2c. The current blackened-dungeon-synth field

Not a formal subgenre in the reference sources, but the acts exist and are
catalogued: Erythrite Throne ("vampyric blackened synth" with symphonic
intonations), Pumpkin Witch (distorted doom-laden guitar tone against synthwave
tactics), Of Ancient Names ("lo-fi melancholic black metal & dungeon synth"),
Inanimus (psych-doom with a dungeon synth member).
[corpus:thedungeonindeepspace, corpus:last.fm/tag/dungeon+synth+black+metal]

The community's own subgenre indices — which list old school, comfy, winter,
chip, forest, sea synth, dark dungeon music, dino synth
[corpus:dungeon-synth.neocities.org/subgenres, corpus:albumoftheyear subgenre
list] — do not name a metal-forward one.

### ⚠ CORRECTED 2026-08-28 — AND THE CORRECTION IS THE OWNER'S

This sheet originally read: *"So 'DS2' is naming a real hole. That is a fair
claim to make in the banner and it is the only claim of novelty this sheet
supports."* **That was wrong, and the owner named the counter-example in one
line:** *"The dinosynth band Synthosaurus uses guitars and distortion so yes
Dinosynth does do the thing you say nothing does."*

He is right. [Synthosaurus](https://synthosaurus.bandcamp.com/album/rise-of-the-synthosaurs)
is dino synth with real guitars and aggressive drums — "Ripping Raptor" is
dinosaurs shredding — across **18 releases** on Bandcamp, tagged dungeon synth
and dino synth. The heavier hybrid is being made, inside a named subgenre, right
now.

**What that costs this sheet, precisely.** The absence of a *label* in a
subgenre index is not the absence of the *music*, and I read the first as
evidence of the second. That is the same error shape this repo already
catalogues — reasoning between two true facts to a mechanism nobody checked.
The two facts were true (the indices really do not list one; the blackened acts
in §2c really are catalogued) and the conclusion between them was not.

**So DS2 claims no novelty.** What survives is stronger and needed no novelty:
the hybrid has working prior art in at least three directions — Summoning doing
it with keyboards in 1995 (§2a), Tänzelcore doing it toward hard dance (§2b),
and Synthosaurus doing it with actual guitars today. DS2 is a well-populated
tradition rather than an empty space, which is a better position to build from
because there is something to measure against.

## 3. THE DOUBLE KICK — WHAT THE SOURCES SAY THE TECHNIQUE IS

The owner asked for double kick pedals specifically, so the tempo the technique
actually lives at matters, and it is measured against ours in §6.

**Tempo bands.** Metal spans "slow ballad tempos (quarter note = 60 BPM) to
extremely fast blast beat tempos (quarter note = 350 BPM)"; grindcore and black
metal "often push well past 200 BPM", while a metalcore breakdown blast sits
nearer 140–160. [corpus:wikipedia/Heavy_metal_drumming, corpus:drumbeatsonline]

**Where the technique changes hands.** This is the most concrete number found:

| tempo | what the foot does |
|---|---|
| up to ~140 BPM | whole-leg motion, gravity drops the beater |
| above ~170 BPM | ankle-dominant strokes — "most of the world's fastest players switch" |
| 200 BPM+ | swivel; Helly plays 16ths at 200, Lombardo's *Angel of Death* ostinato at 210 |

[corpus:wannabedrummer "Mastering the 140-170 BPM range", corpus:tomtommag/Helly]

**A double kick is not a blast beat, and the sources separate them cleanly.** A
double bass roll "uses two feet to play a fast, sustained pattern on the kick
drum **alone**, often under a separate hand pattern", while a blast beat
"distributes the same fast pulse across the kick, snare and cymbal **together**,
creating a fuller wall-of-sound effect rather than just a fast kick pattern".
The blast proper is "a repeated sixteenth-note figure … divided uniformly among
the bass drum, snare, and ride, crash, or hi-hat", usually past 200 BPM.
[corpus:drumbeatsonline, corpus:wikipedia/Blast_beat, corpus:drumming.com]

**DS2 has the first and not the second**, and that is the correct order: the
owner asked for a double kick.

## 4. THE DISTORTION — WHAT THE SOURCES ASK FOR

`the-pedals-are-the-genre.md` already sourced the *circuits* (Meathead, Big
Muff, Fuzz Face, the doom/sludge pedal list) and the speaker bandwidth. What it
does not cover is the **black metal** tone, which is a different target from a
doom fuzz, and DS2 is the genre that wants it:

- **Gain high but not to noise** — "plenty of gain, but not so much that it
  becomes a wall of noise, as clarity and definition are key."
- **Mids scooped, treble up** — "scoop the mids … black metal often features a
  more hollowed-out sound"; "turn your treble all the way up … this music is
  supposed to be brittle and icy."
- **The wall of sound is made by the picking, not the pedal** — tremolo picking
  "generates a wall of sound"; power chords are "bread and butter", with
  diminished and augmented chords for the haunting version, and "moving fifths".
  [corpus:riffhard/black-metal-guitar-tone, corpus:riffhard/how-to-play,
   corpus:nycguitarschool, corpus:guitarmasterymethod]

**Two of those three are already in this program and one is not.** DS2's fuzz
declares `mids` and a `cab` corner per leg (§6), and dungeon synth's harmony
already leans on open fifths (`voicing.open`, `parallels`) — which *is* the
power chord, and the DS002 tutorial transcript in the repo root says so in the
same words: a fifth is "basically just a chord that leaves out the third scale
degree … if you come from a rock guitar school of thought". The missing one is
the tremolo picking, and §6 says why that matters more than the pedal does.

## 5. WHAT THE REPO'S OWN TRANSCRIPTS ADD

Three YouTube transcripts sit at the repo root — `DS001`, `DS002`, `Dinosynth`
— and they are practitioner sources for the *parent* genre, so they are the
control against which DS2's departures should be judged. What they actually say:

- **Loop repetition is the idiom, not laziness.** "In dungeon synth we can
  really get away with looping the same" part; the Dino synth track keeps "the
  same chords repeat throughout the whole song" deliberately, "to keep the track
  extra primitive". Variation arrives by changing "every fourth instance".
- **Reverb past the point of taste.** "I want to live in this track with way too
  much reverb **in the traditional dungeon synth style**."
- **The palette is harp / pad / square-and-triangle chip leads**, with square
  waves chosen because "square waves are really common in old Nintendo games".
- **Percussion is a big single drum, not a kit** — a sampler with "a really
  really beefy attack", pitched, "maximum two voices, like it's two drums", one
  hit on beat one of each bar, layered with extra percussion "to beef the drum
  sound up".
- **Minor keys, and a lo-fi layer over everything** — noise underneath, vinyl
  emulation on the master.

**The last two are the two DS2 pushes hardest against**, and knowingly: one
stomp a bar becomes a 16th double-kick run, and a lo-fi noise floor becomes a
fuzz. Worth having written down that the departure is deliberate and which
sources it departs from.

## 6. WHAT DS2 ACTUALLY DOES TODAY — MEASURED

Build `2026-08-27a`, `GENRE.ds2`, `node harness/mk2_score.js --genre ds2
--seed 1`. 161 bars, 9:45, 11 sections, minor.

**The tempo climax works.** The printout's section tempi:

```
  intro 65 | verse 54 | verse 60 | instrumental 61      the quiet legs
  verse 92 | verse 94 | chorus 100                      the climax
  bridge 58 | verse 57 | chorus 55 | outro 65           the way back
```

That is the `tempoArc` doing what its table claims: `deeper` at 1.42–1.58
against 0.80–0.96 for the other three, normalised so the record does not get
longer. (The printout does not name movements, so the leg boundaries here are
inferred from the tempo profile — there is exactly one contiguous fast run and
it is the right length.)

**The double kick plays, in the climax and nowhere else.** Kick grids counted
off the printout, one row per bar:

```
  quiet legs (47 bars)        climax (40 bars)
  x...x...........   28       x...x...........   13
  x...x.........x.    8       xxxxxxxxx...xxxx    7
  x.x.x...........    5       xxxxxxxxx....xx.    6
  x...x...x.......    4       xxxxx.xxx...xx.x    4
  x...............    2       (5 more 16th variants)  7
                              x...............    3
```

**24 of 40 climax bars carry a sixteenth run; 16 stay on the walk.** Zero of the
47 quiet-leg bars do. So the feature is real, it is confined where the table
says, and it is not a wall — the leg breathes.

**The heavy FX ramp is declared per leg** and it is a blend, not a switch:
`fuzz.amt` 0.22 → 0.34 → **0.68** → 0.18 across the four legs, the Muff's `meat`
0.24 → 0.40 → **0.66** → 0.20, and the cabinet corner opening 3600 → 4800 Hz
into the climax and closing to 3400 on the way home. `g.glide` ramps these
rather than stepping them, which is the "no hard switching" the owner asked for.

**The kit under it:** toms on every bar as the timekeeping layer (the owner's
correction, `a1a41fd`), hat at two strokes a bar, a `wardrum` kick and
`erangDrum` toms — so the double kick is being played by a war drum, not a metal
kick.

## 7. THE GAPS THE SOURCES NAME, IN THE ORDER THEY MATTER

Each of these is a source above disagreeing with the program as it stands. None
is a defect; they are the difference between what DS2 is and what the sources
describe. **The owner's ear decides which are worth closing.**

| gap | the source | what would close it |
|---|---|---|
| **There is no tremolo picking.** It is item one of the three things §1 says dungeon synth removed, and §4's sources say it — not the pedal — is what makes the wall of sound. Every distortion knob in DS2 sits on parts that play whole notes and quarter notes. A fuzz on a sustained pad is a *texture*; a fuzz on a re-articulated 16th line is a *riff*. | [corpus:wikipedia/Dungeon_synth, corpus:riffhard] | A re-articulation option on the comp or ostinato in the climax leg only — one held note becoming n strokes at the sixteenth. Mechanically this is the tom loop's own trick applied to a pitched lane. **Not built, not designed, not costed.** |
| **The climax is at half the tempo the double kick idiom lives at.** 92–100 BPM against 140–210. 16ths at 100 BPM is 6.7 strokes a second, which is real and playable — but the sources put the technique's home two bands above it. | §3 | Either a bigger `deeper` multiplier, or the same kick figure written as 8ths at a doubled feel. A judgement, not a fix — Summoning's own aim was "as slow and bombastic as possible" (§2a), which argues the other way. **This is the one place two good sources genuinely conflict, and the ear should settle it.** |
| **There is no blast beat and no cymbal wall.** The double kick is the feet alone; the sources' "fuller wall-of-sound" needs the snare and cymbal on the same pulse. | §3 | Named only. `2218c88` already measured the opposite move off a reference (hat sparser, cymbal takes over at double time) and it is the better-sourced option of the two. |
| **The dirt is off the drums by design** — `rows` excludes them, on the argument that "a taiko through a fuzz is a different instrument". Defensible, and unmeasured either way. | the table's own comment | An A/B, if the ear says the climax lacks weight. |
| **Nothing is on the record's aesthetic layer.** §5's sources are unanimous about the lo-fi floor and the excessive reverb, and DS2 inherits dungeon synth's, which was built before there was a fuzz in front of it. | §5 | Ask whether the climax's tail is still the parent's cavern once there is a Big Muff in the signal. Unmeasured. |
