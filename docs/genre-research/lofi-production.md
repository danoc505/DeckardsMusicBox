## 1. TEMPO — four sources, four bands

| source | band |
|---|---|
| ModeAudio | **60–80 bpm** |
| Melodigging | **60–90 bpm** (or double-time 120–180 with a half-time feel) |
| EDMProd | **70–100 bpm** |
| Native Instruments / Soundtrap | "around 80 bpm" as a starting point |

The overlap of all four is **70–80 bpm**, and every source contains it. That
band is the only part with unanimous support; anything wider is one source's
opinion.

## 2. HOW MANY THINGS PLAY AT ONCE — the most consistent finding in the sheet

This is the number the sources are clearest and most emphatic about.

> "Many LoFi Hip Hop tracks feature **not more than 3 or 4 elements** picking
> out a **minimal number of notes or chords** in a languid rhythm."
> [corpus:modeaudio, 5 Production Essentials]

And the kit specifically:

> "often featuring **just 3 elements of drums, and not uncommonly just 2**."
> [corpus:modeaudio]

> Most patterns use **3–4 drum elements** — basic boom bap is kick, snare,
> closed hi-hat (3); half-time is kick, snare, hi-hat (3).
> [corpus:transmissionsamples, Lofi Drum Patterns] **`[two sources]`**

EDMProd counts differently and it is worth reading carefully rather than as a
contradiction — its "5 main elements" are **chords/melody, drums, bass, vinyl
crackle/foley, and a sampled vocal or impact FX** [corpus:edmprod]. So: three
things that play notes, plus the kit, plus texture. That is consistent with
ModeAudio's 3–4 if you count only the parts carrying pitch.

The supporting statements all point the same way:

> "Lo-fi compositions are often **simple and minimalistic** in terms of their
> arrangements, focusing more on the atmosphere than anything else."
> [corpus:emastered]

> Melodies should be "**sparse and repeating** to maintain focus and calm."
> [corpus:melodigging]

> "plenty of **space between the drums**, leaving room for a minimalist groove"
> [corpus:melodigging]

> "lofi hip hop thrives on **restraint** — using filtering, panning, and
> **minimal element count** to create breathing room rather than filling every
> frequency and space with sound." [corpus:emastered]

**So the target is roughly: three pitched parts (chords, bass, one melodic
voice), a three-element kit, and texture.** Not a law — a strong and repeated
description of the repertoire.

## 3. THE SHAPE OF A TRACK

> "Think in **vignettes: 1.5–3 minute tracks** with brief intros/outros,
> occasional **dropout sections**, and **minimal variation**."
> [corpus:melodigging]

> "A typical lo-fi hip hop beat might have an **intro, a main loop that builds
> slightly over time**, and sometimes a **breakdown where elements drop out**
> before returning to the core groove." [corpus:soundtrap]

> "lo-fi hip hop typically features **shorter loops that repeat throughout**"
> [corpus:edmprod]

> "Chop short phrases into a stable loop (**1–8 bars**)." [corpus:melodigging]

`[two sources]` on the shape — intro, a loop that barely develops, a dropout,
a return. That is the same architecture `lofi-form.md` already sourced and
built the form plan from, arrived at independently here, which is worth
knowing: the form work does not need revisiting.

The **1.5–3 minute** length is one source. `lofi-form.md` measured the built
songs at 1:55–3:27 and marked the long edge `[EAR]`. This sheet's number
supports the short end and gives no support to anything past 3:00.

## 4. DRUMS

**The pattern.** Kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4, hats filling between
[corpus:transmissionsamples; corpus:hiphopmakers]. EDMProd adds one detail
worth having: "the **second kick for every bar is featured on the offbeat**",
and "an **open hat as the last in the pattern** adds a sense of continuation."

**The feel is the genre, and it is specific:**

> "one element of the kit is often **singled out for off-the-grid note
> placement**, a classic Hip Hop technique." [corpus:modeaudio]

That is a sharper claim than "everything is loose" — ONE element moves.
EDMProd's version: kick and snare "usually are quantized (or close) to the
grid", while the hats swing — "bring the first hat back a bit and the second a
bit forward."

**Numbers, such as they are:**

- **10–15 ms** shifts to start with, "and adjust until it grooves"
  [corpus:transmissionsamples].
- Classic hip hop swing sits **54–58%**, pushed further for a "drunken Dilla"
  flavour [corpus:search summary of the drum-programming results].
- Hi-hat **velocity randomisation 10–20%**, and **timing offsets of 3–8 ticks**,
  to avoid the "typewriter" sound [same].
- Ghost notes and velocity variation for human feel [corpus:melodigging].

**HONEST GAP:** the swing and hat-randomisation figures come from a search
summary spanning several drum-programming pages rather than from one page I
fetched and read. Treat them as indicative, not sourced, until someone pins
them to a document. The 10–15 ms figure IS from a page I read.

## 5. THE EFFECTS — what is actually done to the sound

The texture list is unanimous across every source: **vinyl crackle, tape hiss,
light saturation, bit-depth/sample-rate reduction, and wow & flutter**
[corpus:melodigging; corpus:modeaudio; corpus:landr; corpus:output;
corpus:loopcloud].

**Each one, defined by the sources:**

- **Vinyl crackle** — "**fills in empty space**" [corpus:audioplugin.deals].
  That is a function, not a decoration: it is what makes a sparse arrangement
  sound intentional rather than empty. EDMProd notes it is **low-passed**.
- **Tape hiss** — "another great option" for atmosphere [corpus:modeaudio].
- **Wow and flutter** — "subtle, unpredictable **pitch** modulation that
  simulate changes in tape speed. **Wow creates slow pitch drift while flutter
  creates fast pitch wobble**" [corpus:audioplugin.deals]. And where it is
  heard: "much more noticeable on **sustained sounds like pads or leads**. Even
  subtle settings with slow modulation can give these sources just enough
  shimmering motion" [corpus:landr].
- **Bit/sample-rate reduction** — "simulates a lower sample rate than the
  original", the sound of "early sampling technology"
  [corpus:audioplugin.deals].
- **Saturation** — "Start with **10–20% saturation** so the sound feels thicker
  and more cohesive, **not fuzzy or distorted**"
  [corpus:northernvalleyaudio]. The one concrete amount found anywhere.
- **The low-pass is the signature move, and it is DEEP:** "low pass filtered to
  an usually deep extent… softening the spiky transients", "pulling the cutoff
  down to around **2 kHz or so**" [corpus:modeaudio]. Corroborated in kind by
  "Don't be afraid to roll off highs" [corpus:landr] and "Keep transients soft;
  avoid harsh highs. Low-pass filters and tape emulations help achieve the
  signature warmth" [corpus:melodigging] **`[two sources]`**.

**On the drum bus specifically:** "bouncing your entire drum bus to a stereo
track, then running that through **tape saturation** to give you cohesive,
glued drums that feel like a single instrument" [corpus:northernvalleyaudio];
"Group processing on drums is very important… helps to **glue** the drums
together" [corpus:hiphopmakers] **`[two sources]`**.

**And restraint is stated as often as the effects are:** "**A little bit goes a
long way**" about crackle and noise [corpus:landr]; "making sure not to
overcook things", "you really have to **dial things back** for subtlety", "keep
an eye on the Dry/Wet control and make sure it's set **relatively low**, no
matter the DAW" [corpus:modeaudio]; "leave headroom and **resist heavy
processing** — let the room and noise breathe" [corpus:emastered].

## 6. MIXING

- **High-pass the melodic parts** to clear the low end: "High-pass your melodic
  instruments to leave space for your kick and bass — aim for clarity, not
  thinness." [corpus:emastered]
- **Panning is the space-making tool**: "All elements of the track need space in
  the mix and shouldn't clash. The main method to do this is **panning**…so
  instruments with similar tonal shape can coexist without clashing"
  [corpus:emastered]. And what NOT to pan: "kicks, snares, leads, main chord
  progressions" stay centred; pan "high melody, guitar lines, bells and chimes,
  high drum layers" [corpus:mondoloops] **`[two sources]`** — and this agrees
  exactly with the mono-compatibility rules already in `stereo.md`.
- **Sidechain** to glue kick and bass [corpus:mondoloops; corpus:melodigging].
- **Headroom**: "Prioritize headroom; keep LUFS relatively conservative to avoid
  fatigue"; "warm tonality, restrained brightness" [corpus:melodigging].

## 7. WHERE THE SOURCES CONFLICT — recorded, not resolved

1. **How hard to sidechain.** EDMProd: "you can get away with a **more brutal**
   form of sidechain compression, as it's part of the aesthetic." Melodigging:
   "gentle compression and sidechain to glue drums and samples **without
   pumping**." Directly opposed. Neither is obviously the majority view.
2. **The order of the effect chain.** Output.com: EQ/filter → saturation →
   modulation → tape/vinyl → reverb. ModeAudio: saturation → chorus/tape
   warble → EQ high-cut *last*. The disagreement is real and matters — filtering
   before saturation and filtering after it sound different, because saturation
   generates the very highs the filter is there to remove. Output.com's own
   hedge: "Changing the order of the effects in the chain can lead you to
   different results."
3. **Tempo**, §1 — four bands, overlapping only at 70–80.

## 9. WHAT THE SOURCES DO NOT GIVE

- **No per-track analysis exists that I could find.** I searched for structural
  breakdowns of Nujabes, Jinsang and Idealism tracks — layer counts, arrangement
  maps — and found none. Every source is a general tutorial. So the element
  counts in §2 are tutorial consensus, not a measurement of real records, and
  nobody should upgrade them to "measured" on the strength of this sheet.
- **Almost no numbers for the mixing.** Two of the most professional-looking
  sources (LANDR's mixing guide, Mondoloops' mix-and-master guide) contain
  essentially no numeric parameters at all — no EQ frequencies, no ratios, no
  LUFS. Recorded so nobody re-reads them expecting some.
- **Nothing about how a lofi track ENDS**, beyond "brief outros".
- **Nothing on how the bass is written** beyond "smooth and simple".
